# Preregistration: Who should move the arm?

Committed before any demonstration is recorded and before any scored trial is run.
Any later change to this file or to `analysis/analysis.py` is logged under **Deviations** with a date and a reason.

## Question

On a low-cost arm (SO-101), does a frontier model add more value by writing the motion itself (A) or by pointing for a small learned policy (C), and how do both compare with that policy alone (B)?

## Arms

| Arm | Who decides | Who moves | Demonstrations |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Claude Opus 5.5 (`claude-opus-5-5`), tool-use loop with five tools: `look`, `table_point`, `move_to`, `gripper`, `home` | Inverse kinematics on the SO-101 URDF (or a taught grid if calibration fails G0) | None |
| B | SmolVLA fine-tuned from `lerobot/smolvla_base`, given the instruction in words | SmolVLA | 80 |
| C | Claude Opus 5.5 returns two pixels (grasp, release) from frame 0 and the instruction; the rings are drawn on every scene frame | SmolVLA, same recipe and demos as B, trained on marked frames | 80 |
| C-ACT | Same marks as C | ACT, trained on marked frames, no language input | 80 |

Limits per trial, all arms: 120 s. Arm A additionally: at most 25 tool calls.

## Conditions

| Condition | Scene | Layouts |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | One training object, one container, clean table | 10 |
| E2 | Target plus two training-object distractors; the instruction names the target | 10 |
| E3 | One held-out object plus one distractor | 10 |

The 30 evaluation layouts are generated by `scripts/layouts.py` with seed **20261123** before any arm is run. Held-out objects are chosen and listed in `objects.md` before the first demonstration and never appear in a demonstration.

Each layout is run once by each of A, B, C and C-ACT: **120 scored trials**. The order of the four arms is rotated across layouts.

Week 3 adds **20 more layouts** (same generator, next seed) for B and C only.

## Scoring

- **Success:** the named object is entirely inside the named container, the gripper is open and the arm is clear of the object, at or before 120 s.
- **Progress (0 to 4):** 1 reached (gripper within about 3 cm of the target), 2 holding, 3 carried over the container, 4 released inside.
- **Grasp:** progress of 2 or more.
- **Failure tag:** one of `perception`, `reach`, `grasp`, `transport`, `release`, `freeze`, `none`.
- **Primary scores** come from a blind re-score: scene videos renamed to random ids and scored a day later without the arm label. Live scores are also reported. Disagreements are resolved from the wrist video and reported.

## Hypotheses

| # | Hypothesis | Supported if | Refuted if | Trials used |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Marks beat words off the training distribution | C success exceeds B by at least 25 points | C within 10 points of B, or below it | E2 and E3 (20 layouts) |
| H2 | A learned grasp beats a scripted grasp | C grasp rate exceeds A by at least 20 points | A grasps as often as C or more | All 30 layouts |
| H3 | The agent alone works on the easy case | A succeeds on at least 30% of E1 | A under 15% on E1 | E1 (10 layouts) |
| H4 | Marks carry the task, not the language | C-ACT success within 15 points of C | C-ACT more than 15 points below C | All 30 layouts |

A result between the supported and refuted thresholds is reported as **inconclusive**.

## Statistics

- Success and grasp rates with **Wilson 95% intervals**.
- **Exact McNemar tests** on the paired layouts: B vs C (H1, E2 and E3), A vs C (H2, all), C vs C-ACT (H4, all).
- In a paired comparison, a difference of 2 or fewer between the two discordant counts is called a **tie**, not a win, whatever the verdict. Re-testing one checkpoint on the same placements moved counts by about 2 in the 12-to-200 study.
- With 30 trials per arm, only gaps of about 30 points are resolvable; smaller effects are not claimed.

## Re-runs and exclusions

A trial is re-run only for a hardware fault unrelated to the arm's decisions: camera timeout, USB drop or motor overload. At most one re-run per trial. Every re-run is logged with its reason. Nothing else is excluded.

## Model selection

Checkpoints are saved every 5k steps. Each run's checkpoint is chosen by a 5-trial smoke test on E1-style layouts that are not among the 30 evaluation layouts, never by training loss.

## Gates and kill rule

- **G0** (calibration): mean residual under 1 cm at 9 points, else arm A uses the taught grid.
- **G1** (arm A pilot): at least 2 of 10, or one clear fix; else arm A is frozen as is.
- **G2** (data): the check script reports no flags and 20 marked frames pass a visual audit.
- **G3** (smoke test): B and C each reach at least 2 of 5 on E1, with one retrain allowed.
- **Kill rule:** if B and C are both under 20% on E1 after the retrain, the study stops and is reported as is.

## Fixed versions

- LeRobot at commit `8bf6056d`, no upgrade during the study.
- Claude Opus 5.5, model id `claude-opus-5-5`. System prompt and tool schemas frozen and committed after the arm A pilot.
- SmolVLA from `lerobot/smolvla_base`: batch 64, 20k steps. ACT: batch 8, 30k steps, mixed precision.
- Week 3 brain swap for C: Gemini Robotics ER 2, reported separately.

## Data format

Every scored trial is one row of `results/trials.csv`:

`trial_id, layout_id, condition, arm, run_order, date, scorer, success, progress, failure_tag, duration_s, tool_calls, api_cost_usd, decision_latency_s, rerun_reason, notes`

- `condition`: E1, E2 or E3. `arm`: A, B, C or C-ACT. `scorer`: live or blind.
- `success`: 0 or 1. `progress`: 0 to 4.

All scored trials, failures included, are released as a LeRobot dataset after the results are written up.
