# Preregistration: Who should move the arm?

Committed before any demonstration is recorded and before any scored trial is run.
Any later change to this file or to `analysis/analysis.py` is logged under **Deviations** with a date and a reason.

## Question

On a low-cost arm (SO-101), does a frontier model add more value by writing the motion itself (A) or by pointing for a small learned policy (C), and how do both compare with that policy alone (B)?

## Arms

| Arm | Who decides | Who moves | Demonstrations |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Claude Opus 5.5 (`claude-opus-5-5`), tool-use loop with five tools: `look`, `table_point`, `move_to`, `gripper`, `home` | Inverse kinematics on the SO-101 URDF (or a taught grid if calibration fails G0) | None |
| B | SmolVLA fine-tuned from `lerobot/smolvla_base`, given the instruction in words | SmolVLA | 80 |
| C | Claude Opus 5.5 returns two pixels (grasp, release) from frame 0 and the instruction; the rings are drawn on every scene frame | SmolVLA, same recipe and demos as B, trained on marked frames | 80 |
| C-ACT | Same marks as C | ACT, trained on marked frames, no language input | 80 |

Limits per trial, all arms: 120 s. Arm A additionally: at most 25 tool calls.

## Conditions

| Condition | Scene | Layouts |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | One training object, both containers, clean table | 10 |
| E2 | Target plus two training-object distractors; the instruction names the target | 10 |
| E3 | One held-out object plus one distractor | 10 |

The 30 evaluation layouts are generated by `scripts/layouts.py` with seed **20261123** before any arm is run. Held-out objects are chosen and listed in `objects.md` before the first demonstration and never appear in a demonstration.

Each layout is run once by each of A, B, C and C-ACT: **120 scored trials**. The order of the four arms is rotated across layouts.

Week 3 adds **20 more layouts** (same generator, next seed) for B and C only.

## Scoring

- **Success:** the named object is entirely inside the named container, the gripper is open and the arm is clear of the object, at or before 120 s.
- **Progress (0 to 4):** 1 reached (gripper within about 3 cm of the target), 2 holding, 3 carried over the container, 4 released inside.
- **Grasp:** progress of 2 or more.
- **Failure tag:** one of `perception`, `reach`, `grasp`, `transport`, `release`, `freeze`, `none`.
- **Primary scores** come from a blind re-score: scene videos renamed to random ids and scored a day later without the arm label. Live scores are also reported. Disagreements are resolved from the wrist video and reported.

## Hypotheses

| # | Hypothesis | Supported if | Refuted if | Trials used |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Marks beat words off the training distribution | C success exceeds B by at least 25 points | C within 10 points of B, or below it | E2 and E3 (20 layouts) |
| H2 | A learned grasp beats a scripted grasp | C grasp rate exceeds A by at least 20 points | A grasps as often as C or more | All 30 layouts |
| H3 | The agent alone works on the easy case | A succeeds on at least 30% of E1 | A under 15% on E1 | E1 (10 layouts) |
| H4 | Marks carry the task, not the language | C-ACT success within 15 points of C | C-ACT more than 15 points below C | All 30 layouts |

A result between the supported and refuted thresholds is reported as **inconclusive**.

## Statistics

- Success and grasp rates with **Wilson 95% intervals**.
- **Exact McNemar tests** on the paired layouts: B vs C (H1, E2 and E3), A vs C (H2, all), C vs C-ACT (H4, all).
- In a paired comparison, a difference of 2 or fewer between the two discordant counts is called a **tie**, not a win, whatever the verdict. Re-testing one checkpoint on the same placements moved counts by about 2 in the 12-to-200 study.
- With 30 trials per arm, only gaps of about 30 points are resolvable; smaller effects are not claimed.

## Re-runs and exclusions

A trial is re-run only for a hardware fault unrelated to the arm's decisions: camera timeout, USB drop or motor overload. At most one re-run per trial. Every re-run is logged with its reason. Nothing else is excluded.

## Model selection

Checkpoints are saved every 5k steps. Each run's checkpoint is chosen by a 5-trial smoke test on E1-style layouts that are not among the 30 evaluation layouts, never by training loss.

## Gates and kill rule

- **G0** (calibration): mean residual under 1 cm at 9 points, else arm A uses the taught grid.
- **G1** (arm A pilot): at least 2 of 10, or one clear fix; else arm A is frozen as is.
- **G2** (data): the check script reports no flags and 20 marked frames pass a visual audit.
- **G3** (smoke test): B and C each reach at least 2 of 5 on E1, with one retrain allowed.
- **Kill rule:** if B and C are both under 20% on E1 after the retrain, the study stops and is reported as is.

## Fixed versions

- LeRobot at commit `8bf6056d`, no upgrade during the study.
- Claude Opus 5.5, model id `claude-opus-5-5`. System prompt and tool schemas frozen and committed after the arm A pilot.
- SmolVLA from `lerobot/smolvla_base`: batch 64, 20k steps. ACT: batch 8, 30k steps, mixed precision.
- Week 3 brain swap for C: Gemini Robotics ER 2, reported separately.

## Data format

Every scored trial is one row of `results/trials.csv`:

`trial_id, layout_id, condition, arm, run_order, date, scorer, success, progress, failure_tag, duration_s, tool_calls, api_cost_usd, decision_latency_s, rerun_reason, notes`

- `condition`: E1, E2 or E3. `arm`: A, B, C or C-ACT. `scorer`: live or blind.
- `success`: 0 or 1. `progress`: 0 to 4.

All scored trials, failures included, are released as a LeRobot dataset after the results are written up.

## Deviations

All three entries below were added on 23 Nov 2026, before any demonstration or scored trial.

1. **Lighting (clarification).** Daylight cannot be controlled at this rig. All demonstrations and scored trials are run after dark under room lights, curtains closed. A scene frame is saved at the start of each session and its mean brightness logged.
2. **Containers changed (bowls to a tumbler and a mug).** The two bowls (11 cm across, 7.6 cm tall) are replaced by the pink tumbler (opening 6.8 cm, 6.1 cm tall) and the mug (opening 6.3 cm, 4.8 cm tall). They are named by shape so that no container name shares a colour word with a piece. Reasons: the bowls blocked the wrist camera and shrank the usable table area, and arm A succeeded in 4 of 4 development runs into the bowls once its grasp point was fixed, which suggested the bowl task would not separate the arms. The 10-trial arm A pilot on E1-style layouts (not evaluation layouts) is run on these containers and serves as gate G1. Its result is reported whatever it is; no further change of task is made on the strength of it.
3. **Arm A implementation details (clarification).** Positions given to and reported by arm A refer to the point midway between the open jaws (fingertips 80 mm apart at the default opening), not the fixed finger. Long moves go through a travel height of 110 mm above the table (the taller container is 6.1 cm). The gripper opening is fixed. Fingers are covered with cotton sports tape for friction; the same gripper is used by every arm.

4. **Arm A calibration corrected (instrument).** Added 26 Nov 2026, after the arm A pilot and before any demonstration or scored trial. A spot check on 25 Nov found the arm landing 10 to 30 mm short of marks located with calibration v2: v2 was taken with the fixed fingertip pressed on the table, while trials use the point midway between the open fingertips, in free air. Calibration v3 was taken at that point (a pin taped between the fingers; its position on the gripper was fitted from the 18 touches and confirmed by ruler at 55 mm from the fingertip) over a wider 3 x 3 grid covering the arm's reach within the camera's view; the table height is kept from v2. At two marks not used in v3, the error is now about 7 and 11 mm (v2: 25 to 30 and 10 mm). The fingertip gap at the trial opening is 86 mm, not 80. The arm's model of the wrist roll is off by about 25 degrees; this is not corrected and probably causes most of the remaining error. Arm A uses calib/touch_calib_v3_fit.json from now on. Only arm A uses the calibration; arms B and C are unaffected. Pieces and containers are placed only inside the area enclosed by the 9 calibration points.

5. **Arm A prompt v2 and trial ending (before the freeze).** Added 26 Nov 2026, after the arm A pilot and before any demonstration or scored trial. Arm A's prompt is still unfrozen at this point (frozen after pilot 2). Principle: the prompt gives facts about the robot and its tools, never task strategy or sizes of things on the table, which arm A must judge from its cameras as the other arms do. Removed: the rule to grip across flat faces (unchecked, and wrong for curved pieces), the suggested grasp and release heights, the sizes of the pieces and containers, the travel height in mm (it hinted at container heights; the tool still enforces a safe travel height), and the 25-call limit (arm A-specific; the 120 s limit for every arm stays). Corrected: positions refer everywhere to the point midway between the open fingertips; their accuracy is given as about 1 cm (it was stated as 5 mm); the fingertip gap is 86 mm. Where to click for a position (where an object meets the table) is now separate from what to check before and after release (the container's opening, then its inside). After each trial arm A is asked, with no tools, for a short report (success, first failed step, notes); it is stored as data on self-assessment and shown to the scorer only after the scorer's own score. At the end of a trial the arm goes home and opens its gripper there, so a held piece drops on the table, not into a container; nothing after the trial ends counts. The prompt is arm_a/system_prompt_v2.md; v1 is kept.

6. **Arm A calibration back to v2 (supersedes the calibration choice in deviation 4).** Added 26 Nov 2026, before any demonstration or scored trial. With calibration v3 and the pin positions refitted from its data, arm A's first grasp caught the piece in 1 of 10 development runs; with v2 it did in 3 of 7, and picked the piece up eventually in 6 of 7. The v3 touches were taken with the gripper tilted 25 to 72 degrees and a pin whose position had to be estimated, so they do not describe the poses used in trials. Arm A uses calibration v2 whole and alone; no mixing of calibrations. The v3 files and the development runs stay in the repo (results/dev_a.csv). The 25 Nov spot check's finding that v2 lands short far from the base still stands and is a known limit of the instrument. Pilot 2 (three runs on v3_fit) is void for this reason.
7. **Arm A's final form: prompt v4 and the run_python tool.** Added 26 Nov 2026, before any demonstration or scored trial. Compared with prompt v2 (deviation 5): the gripper is reported as the gap between the fingertips in mm instead of a 0 to 100 reading, which arm A kept misreading as half open; the rule to close the fingers across two flat, parallel faces is reinstated (a strategy hint, by the experimenter's decision after grasps kept failing); and arm A gets a run_python tool that runs its own Python during the trial with the rig, cameras, calibration and kinematics in scope, including direct joint moves. The tool exists because limiting arm A to tools written in advance was judged too restrictive. The 120 s limit includes the time its code runs. Pilot 3 (10 valid trials, E1-style layouts, this configuration): 5 of 10 successes (Wilson 95% interval 24% to 76%); arm A called run_python in only 2 trials. Three runs that crashed (a bug in the new tool, since fixed, and one motor that did not answer at connect) are void and were rerun. Arm A is frozen in this form at the commit tagged arm-a-v1.

8. **Layouts: containers fixed, pieces inside calibration v2's area, demonstration mix, rest pose moved.** Added 26 Nov 2026, before any demonstration or scored trial. The containers stand on their marks (the pencil dots used in pilot 3) in every demonstration and scored trial and are put back on them before each trial. Piece centres lie inside the area enclosed by the 9 points of calibration v2 (this replaces the area named in deviation 4, which was v3's wider grid), at least 10 mm inside its edge, at least 80 mm from either container's centre, at least 70 mm from each other, and clear of the arm at rest as the scene camera sees it. Both containers stand just outside that area, so arm A releases over points it was not calibrated at. Each piece is turned to a random angle (uniform, 0 to 360 degrees). In E3 the distractor is a training object. Each target piece and each container is used about equally often within every condition, and every instruction appears at least once. The 80 demonstration layouts use training objects only (seed 20261125): 40 with the target alone, 20 with one other training object and 20 with both. Seed 20261124 is kept for the week 3 layouts. The order of the four arms rotates by one place from one evaluation layout to the next. Generator: scripts/layouts.py; output: layouts/layouts.json and one reference image per layout in layouts/img/, drawn on calib/layout_base_v2.jpg (the scene with the containers on their marks and no piece). The arm's rest pose, where every trial and demonstration starts and ends, was turned about 82 degrees to the side (shoulder_pan -13.9 to 68.3, other joints unchanged; calib/rest_pose.json), because the folded arm hid part of the calibrated area from the scene camera. Pilot 3 ran from the old rest pose; arm A's prompt and tools are unchanged.

9. **Demonstrations recorded; rest pose as a range; order of arms; no trimming.** Added 27 Nov 2026, after the demonstrations and before any training or scored trial. The 80 demonstrations were recorded on 26-27 Nov with scripts/record_demos.py (overlay and teleoperation in one window). Five episodes (D-28, D-31, D-32, D-33, D-37) lost 1 to 3 video frames to the streaming video encoder and were re-recorded; the final set ankurcw/wmta_demos_v2 holds one episode per demo layout, episode N being layout D-(N+1) (results/demos_v2_index.csv), and passes scripts/check_data.py with no errors. Idle starts are 0.4 to 1.3 s, so the planned trimming of idle lead-ins over 1 s was not done. The rest pose is the folded arm with the base anywhere from 45 to 70 degrees (other joints as in calib/rest_pose.json), as long as it does not block much of the scene camera's view; the demonstrations started with the base at 47 to 60 degrees. Scored trials run B, C and C-ACT first, with their order rotated per layout as before, and arm A's 30 trials after all of theirs (arm A needs no training, and its runs no longer have to fit around training).
